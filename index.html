Where asbestos turns up in Orange buildings

Many houses built in Orange before 1990 used asbestos products, especially fibro extensions, sleepouts and laundries. Orchard and farm sheds around Nashdale, Borenore and Canowindra often have asbestos-cement roofing.

Bonded (non-friable) vs friable

Bonded asbestos is mixed into a material such as cement and is reasonably stable while it's in good condition: fibro sheets, asbestos-cement roofing, pipes and some vinyl floor tiles. More than 10 m² must be removed by a Class A or Class B licensed removalist.

Friable asbestos can be crumbled by hand, such as old pipe lagging, sprayed insulation, some backing on vinyl and badly weathered or fire-damaged sheeting. Any amount must be removed by a Class A licensed removalist.

Common places to find it

  • Walls and eaves: flat fibro sheeting on sleepouts, extensions, eaves and gable ends
  • Wet areas: bathroom and laundry wall linings behind tiles
  • Roofs: corrugated asbestos-cement roofing on houses, sheds and carports
  • Floors: old vinyl tiles and the backing of sheet vinyl
  • Outside: fibro fences, garden sheds and outbuildings
  • Heating: old flue pipes, heater insulation and pipe lagging

Think you've found asbestos? What to do

  1. Stop work and leave it alone. Don't break, drill, cut, sand or water-blast it.
  2. Keep people and pets away from damaged material, and close off the area if you can.
  3. If it's already damaged (storm, fire or a renovation gone wrong), lightly mist loose debris with water to keep dust down, but don't hose it or use a pressure washer. Don't use a household vacuum.
  4. Get it tested. A licensed assessor or competent person can take a sample for an accredited lab.
  5. Use a licensed removalist for anything over 10 m² of bonded asbestos or any friable asbestos. Request a free quote.

Asbestos removal rules in Orange and NSW

Last updated: 2 November 2026

Who can remove asbestos

In NSW, a homeowner may remove up to 10 m² of bonded (non-friable) asbestos themselves, following SafeWork NSW guidance. More than 10 m² of bonded asbestos needs a removalist with a Class B or Class A licence, and any friable asbestos needs a Class A licence. Licensed removalists must notify SafeWork NSW at least 5 calendar days before licensed work starts. You can check a removalist's licence through asbestos.nsw.gov.au or Service NSW.

Transport and tracking

The NSW EPA says a householder who transports more than 100 kg or 10 m² of asbestos waste must report it to the EPA within 24 hours of delivering it. Licensed contractors track loads through the EPA's Integrated Waste Tracking Solution. Asbestos must be wrapped, labelled and taken only to a facility licensed to accept it. See the NSW EPA for current rules.

Local disposal

Orange City Council's Ophir Road Resource Recovery Centre at 261 Ophir Road accepts residential and commercial asbestos when it's wrapped and handled correctly. The council information we found says loads must be booked at least 24 hours ahead on (02) 6393 8861. Contact Orange City Council on 02 6393 8000 to confirm the current booking rules, packaging requirements and fees before you go.

Asbestos can never go in a general skip bin, household wheelie bin or kerbside clean-up.

Asbestos removal in Orange: professional removal vs DIY vs leaving it alone

What NSW rules allow and what is safest if you have asbestos at home in Orange. Source: SafeWork NSW.

Licensed removalist

When it's allowed
Any job. It's required for more than 10 m² of bonded (non-friable) asbestos.
Friable asbestos
Only a Class A licensed removalist can remove friable asbestos, in any amount.
Safety
Trained workers with the right protective gear, and a nominated supervisor for the job.
Paperwork
Licensed work must be notified to SafeWork NSW 5 calendar days before it starts. A clearance inspection by an independent person is required before you move back in.
Disposal
Wrapped and taken to a waste facility licensed to accept asbestos.
Best for
Any amount. SafeWork NSW recommends a licensed professional for any amount of asbestos.

DIY removal

When it's allowed
Only bonded (non-friable) asbestos, 10 m² or less in total.
Friable asbestos
Never. Friable asbestos always needs a Class A licensed removalist.
Safety
You follow the safe removal rules yourself: full protective gear, keeping it wet and intact, and never using high-pressure water or compressed air, which are prohibited on asbestos.
Paperwork
No SafeWork notification for 10 m² or less, but check your council's rules too.
Disposal
You wrap it and take it to a waste facility licensed to accept asbestos.
Best for
Very small bonded jobs, only if you can follow every safety step. SafeWork NSW still recommends a licensed professional.

Leaving it alone

When it's allowed
If the material is in good condition and you won't disturb it. SafeWork NSW says asbestos in good condition poses little health risk.
Friable asbestos
Damaged or crumbly material should be checked by a licensed professional.
Safety
Don't cut, drill, sand or water-blast it.
Paperwork
None.
Disposal
None needed while it stays in place.
Best for
Sound fibro you're not renovating. If you're not sure, get it tested.
