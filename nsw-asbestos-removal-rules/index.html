NSW asbestos removal rules: what applies to your job

Who can remove asbestos in NSW depends on the type and amount. Here's a plain-English summary of SafeWork NSW and EPA rules, written for homeowners and property owners around Orange.

Last checked 24 October 2026

Bonded vs friable asbestos

Bonded (non-friable) asbestos is bound into another material, such as flat or corrugated fibro sheeting, asbestos-cement pipes and some floor tiles. It can't be crumbled by hand when dry. Friable asbestos can be crumbled, pulverised or reduced to powder by hand, such as pipe lagging and some insulation. Bonded asbestos damaged by fire, hail or water-blasting can become friable.

The 10 m² rule

In NSW, SafeWork NSW says a licensed professional must remove any friable asbestos and any amount of bonded (non-friable) asbestos over 10 m², and it recommends a licensed professional for any amount. A single shed roof, a fibro garage or the walls of a bathroom can easily add up to more than 10 m².

Class A and Class B licences

LicenceWhat it covers
Class B (non-friable)Removal of more than 10 m² of bonded (non-friable) asbestos.
Class A (friable)Removal of any amount of friable asbestos, as well as bonded asbestos.

A nominated supervisor must be present whenever Class A work is carried out, and present or readily available for Class B work. Always ask for the licence number and check it on asbestos.nsw.gov.au or through Service NSW before work starts.

Notification before work starts

For licensed removal work (any friable asbestos, or more than 10 m² of bonded asbestos), the licensed removalist must notify SafeWork NSW at least 5 calendar days before starting. They must also give notice to the person who commissioned the work, the occupier and owner of a home, and the immediate neighbours. Emergency removal after a sudden event has a separate phone-and-lodge process.

Transport and disposal

Asbestos waste must be wrapped and taken to a facility licensed to accept it. Under the NSW EPA's WasteLocate system, moving more than 100 kg of asbestos waste or more than 10 m² of asbestos sheeting has to be reported, and facilities that accept asbestos display a WasteLocate plate for transporters to scan. See our Orange disposal guide for local facilities.

What this means for Orange jobs

Typical jobs around Orange include fibro sleepouts and laundries in older homes, asbestos-cement roofing on orchard and farm sheds around Nashdale, Borenore and Canowindra, and vinyl floor tiles. A single fibro shed or a bathroom wall can easily go over 10 m², which makes it licensed work. Orange is covered by Orange City Council; Blayney, Millthorpe, Molong and Canowindra are in Blayney Shire and Cabonne councils, which run their own facilities.
